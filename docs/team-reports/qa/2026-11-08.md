# QA report, 2026-11-08

First run. There is no earlier QA report, so this one covers the board's game code: ships, movement,
power, sensors, weapons, damage, the hangar, the enemy brain, the world and the mission, plus everything
changed since 2026-10-25. It does not cover the close-up view being built today (not yet committed).

## Summary

1. The enemy still plays by its own rules. Its guns need no charge, no arming and no turning, and they aim at where we really are. Its point defence never fires. It is always heard at full reach.
2. The enemy treats a fire on board or a bump into a rock as being shot. It then learns roughly where we are, from anywhere on the map.
3. Fighters land, wait and appear at distances set by the plot's zoom. Zoomed right out, an escort waits 1,316 units away instead of 300.
4. At x10 ships move in bigger steps, so the same turn ends about 320 units from where it ends at x1. Slug hits on a turning ship can be judged wrong.
5. The fight calculator still gives the Garuda our speed, 8 torpedoes (it has 4), weaker armour and weaker hits, and gives us one weak point defence (we have four).

## What I found, worst first

Each finding has: where, what goes wrong, what the player sees, and how sure I am.

### 1. The enemy's weapons skip every rule ours follow

- **Where:** `Assets/Scripts/Tactical/EnemyBrain.cs` lines 132–147 (torpedoes), 214–227 (railguns), 230–240 (aim).
  `Assets/Scripts/Systems/WeaponSystem.cs` lines 852–868 (the enemy's shots are added straight to our incoming list).
  `Assets/Scripts/Tactical/TacticalWorld.cs` lines 550–573 (an enemy ship's charge, lock, guns and point defence are never run).
- **What goes wrong:**
  - Our railgun needs WEP power, a charge of 70, weapons released, four switches, a solution, and a gun that turns at 3° a second (`WeaponSystem.cs` 304, 527–532, 670–679). The enemy's railgun needs none of these. Each turret fires every 12 to 17 game seconds and points at once.
  - The enemy aims at where our ship really is and where it is really going (`EnemyBrain.cs` 232). It only needs to have heard us in the last 30 seconds. It never uses what it heard.
  - Our torpedoes need a scan or a lock (`WeaponSystem.cs` 580). The enemy's torpedoes need nothing. It fires them whenever it is hit, even when it has never heard us.
  - Our point defence shoots at enemy torpedoes (`WeaponSystem.cs` 917–940). Nothing ever shoots at ours. The Garuda's PDC on file (2,400 rounds, `Assets/Ships/Garuda.asset`) is never used.
  - Firing never makes the enemy louder. Ours does (`WeaponSystem.cs` 557, 573).
- **What the player sees:** The Garuda's gun swings onto us instantly. It keeps hitting a moving ship for 30 seconds after we go quiet. None of our torpedoes is ever shot down.
- **This breaks a ruled-out item:** "The enemy is our ship with different numbers and a brain. It never gets its own movement, sensor or weapon rules."
- **How sure:** sure.

### 2. The enemy hears and is heard by its own rules

- **Where:** `EnemyBrain.cs` 166–202 against `Assets/Scripts/Systems/SensorSystem.cs` 84 and 188–198. `TacticalWorld.cs` 158–166 and 550–573.
- **What goes wrong:**
  - The enemy hears us out to its DRADIS reach times our signal. We hear the enemy out to our full reach in any band it gives off, however quiet it is. Its own signal is never worked out, because its sensors are never run.
  - The enemy needs no band. We have to pick one.
  - The enemy's DRADIS gain comes from how alert it is (3, 3, 4, 5). Ours is held down by our SEN power. The enemy's power buses are never filled, because its power is never run. Only its engines are filled at the start and after an EMP (`TacticalWorld.cs` 455, 560).
  - Gas clouds hide only us. The code says so: "Enemies don't hide in clouds yet" (`TacticalWorld.cs` 158).
- **What the player sees:** The Garuda can never go quiet. Cutting its power or putting it in a cloud would change nothing. A cloud works for us and never for it.
- **This breaks a ruled-out item:** the same rule as finding 1 ("never its own sensor rules"). It also breaks "the same rules apply in every level", because clouds work one way only.
- **How sure:** sure.

### 3. Any loss of hull counts as "shot", and tells the enemy where we are

- **Where:** `EnemyBrain.cs` 118–123 (a hit is any fall in armour plus room integrity), 186 and 193.
  The causes: `Assets/Scripts/Systems/DamageSystem.cs` 297–298 (a fire takes 1 integrity every 30 seconds) and `TacticalWorld.cs` 395–401 (crashes).
- **What goes wrong:**
  - A fire burning on the Garuda counts as a fresh hit every 30 seconds. So does a crash into a rock, a wreck or another enemy ship.
  - Each "hit" makes it ALERT, which also starts the reinforcements' countdown (`Assets/Scripts/Game/QuestSystem.cs` 275).
  - It learns where our ship is now, give or take 15% of the distance (`EnemyBrain.cs` 193). It learns this from anywhere on the map.
  - It fires a torpedo salvo if we are within 3,000 units.
  - Its give-up clock starts again with each "hit", so it never calms down while the fire burns.
  - The note on line 193 says "it knows the way the shot came, not the range". The code gives a random spot inside a circle round our real position, so it learns the range too. And it uses where we are now, not where the shot came from.
- **What the player sees:** Our HE torpedo starts a fire on the Garuda. After that, every 30 seconds the Garuda knows where we are. It hunts us and fires torpedoes, even after we have moved away and gone quiet.
- **How sure:** sure that the code does this. Whether fires are common in a real fight needs checking. An HE warhead through an open side starts a fire 60% of the time (`Assets/Ships/Asura.asset`, `fireChanceHE`).

### 5. At x5 and x10 ships move in big steps; torpedoes and fighters don't

- **Where:** `Assets/Scripts/Systems/NavSystem.cs` 171–221 runs once per board tick. A tick is 0.42 game seconds at x1 and 4.2 at x10 (`BoardManager.cs` 235). Torpedoes and fighters move in 0.05-second steps (`WeaponSystem.cs` 704, `HangarSystem.cs` 277).
- **What goes wrong:**
  - At x10 the bow swings a whole tick's turn at once (84° for our ship, 59° for the Garuda). Then the ship moves 4.2 seconds on the new heading. I ran the same 90° turn at full speed for 30 game seconds, using the movement rules and the numbers in `Asura.asset` and `Garuda.asset` (script in the appendix):

    | Ship | x1 ends at | x5 ends at | x10 ends at | x10 off from x1 |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | Asura | (930, 633) | (1056, 542) | (1168, 421) | about 318 units |
    | Garuda | (660, 492) | (752, 433) | (878, 360) | about 255 units |

  - The slug hit test assumes the enemy keeps last tick's course and speed through the whole tick (`WeaponSystem.cs` 755–757). But the enemy turns first and then moves (`TacticalWorld.cs` 565–566). At x10 the Garuda can turn 59° inside one tick, while a slug crosses 1,260 units. The test can place the Garuda tens of units from where it really is. The Garuda's hit box is only 62.5 units from its centre line to its side.
  - The enemy thinks once a tick, so at x10 its railgun can fire only every 4.2 seconds. The reload of 12–17 seconds rounds up to the next tick: about 2 seconds slower on average (guess).
  - The point defence checks a torpedo once per tick, along a straight line from where the torpedo started that tick (`WeaponSystem.cs` 921). A torpedo curving hard inside a 4.2-second tick can pass its check point unseen.
- **What the player sees:** The same moves give a different fight at x10. Turns are tighter, slugs aimed at a turning Garuda hit or miss by different rules, and the Garuda fires a little less often.
- **How sure:** sure for the turn numbers (run). Likely for the slug test and the point defence (worked out, not run).

## Appendix: the two runs

Both were run with `python3`. The numbers come from the files named above.

Turn at x1, x5 and x10 (mirrors `NavSystem.cs` 180–221 at full ENG and a whole engine room):

```python
import math
def run(dt, top, acc, brk, drift, drag, turn, T=30.0, order=90):
    x=y=0.0; hdg=0; vx,vy=0.0,top; bank=0.0; t=0.0
    while t < T-1e-9:
        if hdg != order:
            bank += turn*dt; step=int(math.floor(bank+1e-9))
            if step>=1:
                bank-=step; d=((order-hdg+540)%360)-180
                hdg = order if abs(d)<=step else (hdg+(1 if d>0 else -1)*step)%360
        else: bank=0
        h=math.radians(hdg); nx,ny=math.sin(h),math.cos(h)
        fwd=vx*nx+vy*ny; side=vx*ny-vy*nx
        keep=drag**dt; fwd*=keep; side*=keep*drift**dt
        up = fwd>=0 if top>fwd else fwd<=0
        s=(acc if up else brk)*dt
        fwd = top if abs(top-fwd)<=s else fwd+math.copysign(s,top-fwd)
        vx=fwd*nx+side*ny; vy=fwd*ny-side*nx
        x+=vx*dt; y+=vy*dt; t+=dt
    return x,y
ships={'Asura':(50,2,3.75,0.9143,0.99406,20),'Garuda':(35,1.4,2.6,0.9143,0.99406,14)}
for name,p in ships.items():
    for rate in (1,5,10): print(name, rate, run(0.42*rate,*p))
```
