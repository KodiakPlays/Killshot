# Player 1, 2026-11-08

First run. The fight calculator (`docs/scale-sim/scalesim.py`) is rebuilt and now reads the game's files
itself. Every number here comes from a run of it, a game file, or a sum shown below. Guesses say "guess".

## Summary

1. Wolf Hunt is lost more often than won: a good player wins 21–50% at x1 and 0–4% at x5. An expert wins 25–54% at both rates. A new player never wins.
2. The fight turns on which side the Garuda shows. Its reactor is behind its PORT side, and a good player gets that side first in only 4–17% of fights. Hits on a room that is already wrecked do nothing.
3. At x5 a lock lasts 2 real seconds, too short for anyone but an expert to arm a shot. The plot is slower than the Garuda's reload, and its heading flips near 1,600 units, so a good player gets 1 to 3 shots a fight.
4. Strategies that win far more often than the rest: ACTIVE SCAN on contact in COLD RUN and INTERCEPT (92–100% against 21–75%), firing straight at it in NO PEEKING (100%), torpedoes after a scan for experts at x5 (56%).
5. Nose gun (not built): a slug at 1,500 a second takes 7, 13 and 20 s to fly 10,000, 20,000 and 30,000. It hits 97%, 82% and 65%, held back mostly by whole-degree turning. The Garuda can't hear past 7,500, so a flare up to 7,500 changes nothing; a 15,000 flare leaves half the reach safe.

## What I found

### Wolf Hunt (Level_01)

24 fights a case (48 where marked †). Times are game seconds from the start.

| | first contact | contact to first shot | win% fighter / scan / fire / plot / close / quiet | kill | died% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| new x1 | 212 | 84–142 | 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 | – | 100 |
| new x5 | 307 | never fires | 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 | – | 100 |
| good x1 | 236 | 94–185 | 4 / 25 / 33 / 21 / 38 / 50 | 474–846 | 50–100 |
| good x5 | 285 | 156–258 | 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 4 | 706–1,017 | 100 |
| expert x1 | 233 | 86–183 | 29 / 42 / 48† / 25 / 50 / 29 | 367–564 | 42–75 |
| expert x5 | 262 | 105–204 | 33 / 38 / 38 / 54 / 35† / 42† | 559–688 | 42–83 |

- **Level size and time to finish.** The map is 20,000. A won game ends 480–865 game s in (median 633,
  43 wins): about 4 minutes to find it, 2 to 5 minutes of fighting, then 3 to 5 minutes (171–286 s) to
  reach the jump point.
- **First contact** comes 212–307 s in. By hand: 16,000 − 6,250 DRADIS = 9,750 to close at 37.5
  (ENG 3), plus 15 s setup: 179–275 s, depending on how fast the Garuda comes our way. The maths says 236.
- **Setting up** (contact to first shot) is 1.5 to 3 minutes for good and expert players at x1. The
  draft target is 30 s to 2 minutes, so most first moves fit and quiet and fighter run over.
- **The Garuda always finds us** (found 100%). Its eyes reach 2,000, the same as our railgun, so we can't
  fire unseen.
- **Shots and hits.** A good player at x1 fires 10–20 slugs and hits with 97–100% of them. Only 0.3–3
  of them hit the reactor face. The Garuda fires 21–42 slugs and hits with 93–97%.
- **Dodge chance: 0–2%** in every case. Worked by hand: at 1,500 the Garuda's ±3° spreads its slugs ±79
  units. Our box side-on is ±156. We must be side-on to shoot, so every aimed slug hits. End-on it
  would be 40%.
- **Damage taken:** 190–260 armour and room points by the end for good and expert players, mostly the starboard side and hangar.
- **Dead time.** The longest stretch with nothing to do is 106–122 s, all in the search before contact.
  The only things that break it are the Garuda's radio messages, every 2 minutes. After contact the
  longest is 19–96 s, often the run to the jump point. Both are over the draft 30 s.
- **Why fights are lost (good, x1):** 54–71% die before the kill, 0–25% after it (frigates on the way to
  the jump point). Frigates arrive 180 s after the Garuda's alarm, and most fights last longer than that.

**Finding 1. The reactor face decides the fight.** The Garuda closes at full speed to 1,600 (0.8 of its
2,000 reach), bow on, then turns whichever side needs the smaller turn. Our own direction of travel at
that moment decides which. A player holding it on the starboard gun and moving forward drifts to its
starboard side, so it shows STBD. The good player got PORT first in 4–17% of fights, the expert (who
backs toward its port side in reverse) 17–29% at x1 and 62–83% at x5. Hits on STBD wreck its hangar
in 6 slugs. After that a wrecked room "takes no more", so further hits do nothing. Both ships usually
show STBD, so neither can hurt the other's reactor. The fight ends when slugs run out (22 per gun), or
by fire and vacuum spreading, or when someone turns their PORT side. In the run, a player who switches to
the port gun when the starboard one is empty loses its reactor in 4 hits.

**Finding 2. Breaches spread fast.** A slug through an open side breaches the room half the time.
The vacuum then spreads at 1% a second into each neighbour. Within about two minutes the magazine
(no firing below 3) and the engine room are often airless. In one traced fight our ship couldn't fire
for 100 s while damage control sealed and refilled rooms one at a time.

**Finding 3. x5.** A lock fills in 10 game s and holds for 10 game s, which is 2 real seconds at x5.
Arming takes 40, 20 and 10 game s at x5, so only an expert fits, and then only just. Everyone else must
use POINT, RULER and TRACK. In Wolf Hunt that fails for a second reason. Near 1,600 the Garuda flips
between charging (bow on) and turning side-on every tick or two, because there is no gap between the two
rules. A straight-line plot is out of date before the person finishes arming. A good player gets 1–3
shots a whole fight at x5, against the Garuda's 45–50. Damage control and every other hand action is also
five times slower in game time. A 30 s or 45 s lock hold doesn't help (0–6% wins, 16 fights each).

**Finding 4. Old courses spoil a lock.** A lock leads the shot with the newest scan or plot, however old.
A scan from the approach (cruising at 22) leads the slug the wrong way once the Garuda is side-on at 14.
The calculator's good and expert players scan again or ERASE before locking.

**Finding 5. A still ship reads as moving.** Two POINTs 15 s apart with a 25-unit click error read a
still Garuda at about 2 units a second (FirstShot, traced). TRACK then walks the aim off about 1° every
20 s, and the shots miss after the fourth. A person sees MISS and plots again; the calculator's player now
does.

**Finding 6. Fighters on DESTROY.** Six fighters take 60 armour a run, every 30 s, off whatever face
looks toward them. Each run counts as a hit on the Garuda, so it goes ALERT and learns where our ship
is (within 15% of the distance), from anywhere. Fighters were the worst first move for good players
(4%).

**Finding 7. Torpedoes always reach.** The Garuda's PDCs never fire (QA), so every torpedo that reaches
it hits. HE takes 100 off a face and starts fires 60% of the time once a face is open. A scan, then
torpedo salvos from 2,900, then the railgun: expert 62% at x1 and 56% at x5, good 31% and 6%
(`--extras`, 16 fights each).

**Finding 8 (from the code, not run).** Slugs only test ships' boxes (`WeaponSystem.FirstStruck`), so
they pass through rocks. A rock blocks the Garuda's eyes but not our slug.

### Best order of steps (Wolf Hunt, x1, from the runs)

1. ENG 3, SEN 3, DRADIS on, C2, two or more PDC groups on AUTO. Decode the radio and fly down its bearing.
2. On contact, stay quiet (signal 60 or less). Quiet and close won most often for good players (50%, 38%).
3. Scan once, so hit reports name the room. Scan again, or ERASE the plot, before each lock if the course is over about 40 s old.
4. Keep it on the starboard gun. When it comes at you bow on inside 2,000, back away toward its port side so it turns its PORT face to you.
5. Lock, arm, fire twice per lock. Six PORT hits kill it.
6. If your hits go into a wrecked hangar, break off past 2,600 for 35 s and come in again. Don't switch to the port gun.
7. Experts: torpedo salvos from 2,900 before closing.
8. After the kill, run for the jump point. Frigates arrive 3 minutes after it found you.
