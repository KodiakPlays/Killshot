# Player 1, 2026-11-09

A rerun after Steps 170 and 171 (commit a674637): the spinal railgun, the slow turn, slugs that keep their ship's motion,
no fighters on our ship, and the new level LONG SHOT. Every number here comes from a run of `docs/scale-sim/scalesim.py`,
a game file, or a sum shown below. Guesses say "guess".

## Summary

1. Wolf Hunt got harder. A good player at x1 wins 4–21% (was 21–50%), an expert 21–46% (was 25–50%). At x5 nobody wins (the expert won 38–54%). We now face the Garuda nose first, its hits land on our PROW, and the magazine behind it stops our railgun for 38–63% of the fight.
2. LONG SHOT: scan or plot first wins 96–100% at x1. Firing straight at it, closing or going quiet wins 0%. Those players come in on its starboard side and spend all 12 slugs and 16 torpedoes on a hangar that is already wrecked. The side decides it, not the aim.
3. Long shots: a slug takes 6.7, 13.3 and 20 s to fly 10,000, 20,000 and 30,000. A lock hits 97–100% at every range and allows for our drift, but a lock needs DRADIS, which reaches 7,500 at most. So no shot past 7,500 is possible today. By hand: 96%, 83%, 65%. 10 units a second of drift costs a hand-aimed shot 4–11 points. Stopping and turning onto the aim takes 18–20 s.
4. The training levels hardly moved. WEAPONS FREE is still won every time, a little faster at x1 and slower at x5. FINDERS KEEPERS stays 62%. COLD RUN and INTERCEPT are unchanged. The gun isn't used there.
5. Firing straight at it is never the best choice in Wolf Hunt or LONG SHOT. It still wins every NO PEEKING fight for good and expert players. Shift turns the ship only once a board tick: 0.84° steps at x1, 4.2° at x5.

A lock lands the nose to the part of a degree, so only a turn the Garuda makes while the slug flies spoils it. On a
straight leg, as in LONG SHOT, a lock hits 100% at every range. By hand the person stops on a whole Shift step, so the aim
is off by up to 0.42° at x1 and 0.5° at x5 (±220 and ±260 units at 30,000). The slow turn doesn't help a hand-aimed shot
much, because Shift moves only once a board tick. **But a lock needs the Garuda on DRADIS, 7,500 at most. Today nothing past
7,500 can be seen or locked**, so the 10,000–30,000 rows wait for other eyes (the plan's drones). And even then a lock
can't start past 9,412, where the Garuda no longer fills the sight at MAG 8 (see "Longer DRADIS reach" below). So the
"lock" column past 9,412 needs a higher MAG too; the "by hand" columns are what is possible today.

**Leftover drift** (any direction, when the slug leaves):

| Drift a second | Off the line at 30,000 | Lock 10k / 20k / 30k | By hand 10k / 20k / 30k |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 100 / 99 / 97 | 97 / 83 / 66 |

| 5 | up to 100 | 100 / 99 / 97 | 95 / 80 / 63 |
| 10 | up to 200 | 100 / 99 / 97 | 93 / 72 / 56 |

The lock's lead allows for our own motion (`LeadBearing`), so drift costs a locked shot nothing. The line on the plot runs
along the nose and doesn't show the drift, so a hand-aimed shot pays for it: 10 a second costs 4 points at 10,000 and
10–11 at 20,000–30,000. Below 2 a second it makes no difference.

**Stopping and turning onto the aim** (a 90° turn, game seconds):

| | Brake first, then turn | Turn first |
|---|---|---|
| ENG 5 (50 a second) | 13 s braking + 5 s turning = 18 s | on in 5 s but sliding 29 a second; Q and E stop it 5 s later (10 s). Left alone, the slide takes 43 s to fall below 0.5. |
| ENG 3 (37.5) | 13 + 7 = 20 s | on in 7 s, sliding 18; Q and E: 11 s. Left alone: 38 s. |

The fastest order is to turn first, then hold Q or E against the slide. The last part of a degree takes under 0.5 s at
2° a second.

## Best order of steps (from the runs)

1. Get a course first: ACTIVE SCAN, or POINT and RULER. Without one you can't tell which side to shoot from.
2. Go round to its port side (its reactor) at the stand-off range. In LONG SHOT this is the long part, 5 to 9 minutes.
3. Turn, then stop. Hold Q or E until the slide stops.
4. Set the four switches *before* sighting. A held lock's end clears them, a lock that hasn't started doesn't.
5. Sight, lock, let the ship turn itself, pull the lever as soon as the nose stops.
6. Wolf Hunt: shoot before it reaches you. Once it is close its hits on your nose stop the gun. Keep damage control on
   the magazine.
7. Don't shoot into a room that's already wrecked. 12 slugs is 6 for the kill and 6 to spare.

**How far a lock, DRADIS and the plot reach (from the code):**

- **DRADIS** hears a ship inside base + gain × step, less rock interference (`SensorSystem.SenRangeUnits`, `Heard`). ACTIVE
  SCAN's cone has the same reach (`ConeCovers`).
- **A lock** starts only on a ship DRADIS hears (`ShipState.ScopeSubject`) that fills the sight's brackets
  (`ShipState.ScopeFits`): MAG × 1,000 / range must be 0.85–1.15, and MAG goes from 1 to 8 (`ShipState.SetZoom`). So a lock
  starts no further than 8 × 1,000 / 0.85 = **9,412**. Once held, it lasts its 10 s while DRADIS still hears the ship
  (`WeaponSystem.TickLock`). Today this never matters (DRADIS stops at 7,500). At 12,500 or 15,000 it does.
- **The plot.** POINT puts a mark wherever you click, and marks stay put (`ShipState`, "POINT"). But the plot draws the
  Garuda only where DRADIS heard it or the scan's cone covered it (`PlotPanel`, the sweep's `See`). Past DRADIS there is no
  dot to click on. The radio intercept gives a rough direction (8 ways), heading and speed, not a place.
- **So, plainly:** past DRADIS you can't see the Garuda, so you can't POINT on it, so you can't plot its path. A sniper
  shot past DRADIS needs the sensor drones. Inside DRADIS you lock and fire, out to its edge (owner's rule), so the
  sight must zoom far enough for that.
