## Summary

1. Four things win too often: ACTIVE SCAN on contact in the chases (92–100%), a shot with no course inside 1,875 units (it can't miss), torpedoes (the Garuda's PDCs never fire), and shooting from behind a rock (it blocks the Garuda's eyes, not our slugs; from the code, not run).
2. A fix on the board for each: listening buoys that hear a scan but not a quiet ship, picket frigates small enough that aiming matters again, escorts with PDCs, and rocks that stop slugs.
3. Fleet mode, still one seat: a captured ship you order by radio to make noise. The Garuda turns side-on to it, so you can come at its PORT side. Today a good player gets PORT first in 4–17% of fights; about 41% is needed for 50% wins (P2).
4. New on the board: radiation belts (false contacts, open rooms leak), hot wrecks that only show on THERM, and a freighter lane where matching their signal is the disguise the spec calls the centre of the game.
5. The nose gun (under discussion) can't kill from range by the sums: hits on a bow never reach the reactor. But from 15,000 it stops the Garuda's guns before it arrives, if a shot takes 45 s or less (guess). How strong that is depends on how fast the Garuda repairs its magazine.

## 2. Ideas

Each idea: what the player sees and does, the rules it uses that the game already has, the problem it fixes,
its size, a quick sum, and the toy test (a few clear steps, a moment of commitment, the Red October tension).

### Line A · Several ships on each side

#### A1 · Picket frigates (medium)

The Garuda doesn't sail alone. Two Naga frigates each patrol a route, 3,000–4,000 units ahead of it.

1. Two red bars show on DRADIS before the Garuda's. They move faster than it would.
2. You choose: slip between them, or use one.
3. Any hit wakes a frigate. A frigate that wakes calls the Garuda, which goes SEARCHING toward where the
   frigate heard you.
4. So the call is bait. Hit the picket from beside a gas cloud. Slip into the cloud. Wait with the
   railgun ready for the Garuda to come and look.
5. To hit a moving frigate at all past 312–852 units, you need its course: POINT, POINT, RULER, TRACK, or a
   scan it may hear.

- **Rules it uses.** The frigate and its brain (`Frigate.asset`, the reinforcements). Patrol routes on the
  Level map. `EnemyBrain.Brief` already sends a ship SEARCHING toward a spot. Gas clouds.
- **Fixes.** S2: aiming matters again against frigates. Dead time before contact (106–122 s, P1): the
  pickets are heard 3,000–4,000 units of route sooner, about 80–107 s at ENG 3 (37.5). S3, once QA's PDC fix
  is in.
- **Size.** Medium. Frigates today come only as reinforcements, so a level needs to place them at the start.
  "A woken frigate calls the Garuda" is a few lines on top of `Brief`.
- **Sums.** Can't-miss range = half length × slug speed ÷ target speed, at any heading. Frigate (100 × 25,
  hit area +25%, half length 62.5): 312 at 60, 391 at 48 (SEARCHING), 852 at 22. Four of our slugs on its PORT
  side kill it: one opens its 50 armour (our slug does 50), then 2 a hit on a reactor of 5 (`Asura.asset`).
  The ambush: in a cloud at signal 60 with a lock (+35), the Garuda hears us only inside 1,781 and sees us
  only inside 400 (P2).
- **Toy test.** Three steps: hit, hide, wait. The commitment is the first hit. The tension is the wait in the
  cloud while its bar swings toward you.

#### A2 · The freighter lane: the disguise in its simplest form (large)

A third side. Neutral freighters fly a lane across the map at about 30, each loud, signal 70 (guess).

1. DRADIS shows a line of slow contacts, all on one heading.
2. Far from the Garuda, ACTIVE SCAN one. It reads FREIGHTER and its signal, 70.
3. Set your power to match: ENG 3, WEP 3, SEN 1 is 7 units, signal 70.
4. Join the lane. Same heading, same speed.
5. The Garuda hears you and reads a freighter. It doesn't climb toward ALERT.
6. You choose where to leave the lane: on its PORT side, with WEP already full. Inside 2,000 its eyes see
   you, and the disguise breaks ("getting too close", spec Rule 5.2.c). A lock or a scan breaks it too.

- **Rules it uses.** Signal (10 a unit on any bus). ACTIVE SCAN, which the spec says returns a ship's signal
  (Rule 5.2.b). `EnemyBrain.Listen`, with one new check: a ship that matches a freighter's signal, heading and
  speed is not raised. Patrol routes for the freighters. The GDD's broadcast frequency (GDD p.35) can come
  later.
- **Fixes.** The face: you pick the angle while it doesn't know you are hostile. The Garuda always finds us
  (found 100%, P1). Setting up (contact to first shot 94–185 s, P1): you arrive with WEP full. Scanning gets a
  job beyond the course (P2: a scan adds nothing to a slug).
- **Size.** Large. Neutral ships are new (the prototype had them only as decoration, spec §16). So are the
  match rule and its tolerance.
- **Sums.** Without the disguise, signal 70 is heard by the calm Garuda at 6,250 × 0.7 = 4,375. It goes ALERT
  at half that distance, 2,188: before you reach railgun range. With the disguise you reach 2,000 with WEP 3
  already drawn. ENG 3 tops at 37.5, so matching 30 is easy.
- **Toy test.** Four steps: scan, match, join, break off. The commitment is leaving the lane. The tension is
  riding the lane past the Garuda, loud and armed, hoping the costume works. This is the GDD's "using that
  data to disguise yourself as an enemy ship".

### Line B · Fleet mode (you still fly one ship)

The GDD says "You command a single ship". So here fleet mode means radio orders to a second ship. You never
fly it and never change seats.

#### B1 · The prize decoy (medium)

A ship you capture with SECURE stays where it stopped (spec §12.2). New: it takes one order by radio.

1. Capture a frigate. A level can place a crippled one to take.
2. Fly 3,000–5,000 away from it, round to the far side of where the Garuda will come.
3. COMMS: pick the prize, NOISE, SEND. The prize powers every bus.
4. Go quiet. If the Garuda hears you at all, it comes for you, not the prize. That is today's rule for our
   fighters.
5. The Garuda hears the prize, closes, and turns side-on to it at 1,600 to fire. Its other face points away
   from the prize, toward you.
6. Watch which face that is: the scan dot's heading, or the model once in sight. PORT: come in and fire. STBD:
   wait for it to turn, or torpedo the PORT face from the prize's side.
7. The prize dies, and its prize crew with it.

- **Rules it uses.** SECURE. `EnemyBrain.Listen` already hears our fighters as separate loud things
  (fighter signal 60); the prize is one more. The Garuda's side-on turn. COMMS. Power.
- **Fixes.** The face. Today good players get PORT first in 4–17% of fights (P1); 50% wins needs about 41%
  (P2). With the decoy, the face away from it is PORT about half the time (guess: coming straight at the
  prize, both turns are 90°), and the player sees which before committing. It also adds a first move for good
  players, who have 2 real choices today (P2).
- **Size.** Medium. The Garuda only ever shoots at our ship today (`EnemyBrain.Shoot`), so it must learn to
  shoot at another ship.
- **Sums.** The Garuda hears a signal-100 prize at 6,250. It goes SEARCHING at 4,688 and ALERT at 3,125
  (hearing clarity 0.25 and 0.5). At 22, 28 and 35 it reaches 1,600 in 71 + 56 + 44 = **170 s**. Then, if the
  prize's PORT faces it, three Garuda slugs kill it (75 opens its 50 armour, then 3 a hit on 5), about 29 s at
  one turret's 14.5 s reload. If its STBD faces it, the hangar wrecks and later hits do nothing (P1, finding 1);
  the turret's 22 slugs last 319 s. So the window is about 3 to 8 minutes. Six PORT hits kill the Garuda, and a
  good player fires 2 shots per 20 s lock (P1). That fits.
- **Toy test.** Capture, place, NOISE, strike. The commitment is the prize crew. The tension is going dark
  while something else takes the Garuda's fire. For the nose-gun ship, which carries no fighters, this is its
  decoy.

#### B2 · A sister ship by radio (large)

The level starts with two ships: ours and a second, smaller one (frigate numbers). Its brain is the Garuda's,
on our side. That keeps the rule: one ship model, different numbers, a brain.

1. POINT where you want it.
2. COMMS: pick the ship, pick an order: STOP (power down), GO TO (the newest POINT), NOISE (power up), ENGAGE
   (fire control's target).
3. SEND. The lamp reads SENT, then ROGER.
4. Watch its dot.

**The cost.** Every order is a radio message, by the same rule as the Garuda's to us. The enemy hears it
anywhere. Once it decodes it (guess 20–30 s), it learns which way the sender is, its heading and its speed.
So each order spends a little cover. Radio silence is a choice.

- **Rules it uses.** The enemy brain. Comms (`CommsSystem.Report` already gives bearing, heading and speed).
  POINT. Power.
- **Fixes.** The face: with our two ships on opposite sides of the Garuda, whichever face it shows one ship,
  the other ship sees the opposite face. One of us gets PORT every time. The search: two DRADIS circles cover
  the map faster, which cuts the 106–122 s of dead time before contact (P1) (guess: roughly half).
- **Size.** Large. An allied brain, a target choice for the Garuda, orders on COMMS, the enemy decoding our
  messages.
- **Sums.** The second ship has frigate armour (50 a side): three Garuda slugs on its PORT kill it, about 29 s
  once in reach. It is precious, not a tank.
- **Toy test.** Plan, send, wait, see it work. The commitment is SEND, because the enemy hears it. The tension
  is the silence after: did it decode us?

### Line C · New things on the board

#### C1 · Listening buoys (medium)

Small enemy listening buoys that never move. They are the enemy's version of the sensor drones in the nose-gun
plan, so it is one rule both ways. The GDD's old Imperial records can mark a few, unverified (GDD p.7: "a
listening post").

1. Flip DRADIS to EM. Buoys give off EM only. The Garuda gives off THERM and RAD (spec §9.1). On EM you hear
   buoys but not the Garuda. You can't listen for both at once.
2. A buoy shows as a red bar. It never moves; its bearing changes only because you do. Two COMPASS lines from
   two places cross on it.
3. Keep clear. A buoy hears you by the Garuda's rule: its reach times your signal.
4. When it hears you, it calls the Garuda, which goes SEARCHING toward the spot.
5. Or shoot it. Its going silent calls the Garuda to the buoy, not to you. Bait again.

- **Rules it uses.** DRADIS bands (today every enemy gives off THERM and RAD, so the band switch has little
  to do). The enemy's hearing rule. `Brief`. Rock interference (500 off its reach per rock on the line).
- **Fixes.** S1: near a buoy, a scan stops being free. Dead time: a new buoy coming into hearing is something
  new to learn. COMPASS, never taught (Learning), gets a job.
- **Size.** Medium. A still contact with EM only, the hearing check, the call.
- **Sums.** A buoy at gain 5 reaches 7,500. It hears a ship at signal 50 (COLD RUN power) at 3,750, at 60 at
  4,500, and a scan (85) at **6,375**. We first hear the buoy at 6,250 (SEN 3). So the moment you hear a buoy,
  you are already inside the range where it would hear a scan. Quiet travel is safe past 3,750. Guess: where
  the corridor passes a buoy, the scan's win rate falls toward the plot's 46–75% (P1). Player 1 case 1.
- **Toy test.** Flip to EM, plot it, steer round it. The commitment is the scan you no longer make for free.
  The tension is the classic one: you know it is listening.

#### C2 · Radiation belts (medium)

The owner's later idea (`BUILD_LOG.md`, "For later talks": interference from radiation, false dots for us and
for the enemy). A box, like a gas cloud, round a broken reactor or a dead star.

Rules inside, for every ship, in every level:

- DRADIS shows false contacts, the owner's "false dots". Here they are extra red bars that come and go. A real
  ship is there again on the next sweep; a false one is gone in 8 s.
- The enemy hears false contacts too, and goes to look, as it did in Step 151.
- A room behind an open side (armour 0) leaks radiation while the ship is in the belt. This is the radiation
  condition that already exists: one crew lost every 20 s, spreading 1% a second. Whole armour keeps it out.
  The GDD's words: once a side is open, the rooms are "subject to... cosmic radiation".
- Signal is not cut. A belt confuses; it doesn't hide. That keeps it different from a cloud.

1. A patch of the DRADIS circle flickers with bars.
2. Which one is the Garuda? Watch one more sweep. The one that stays is real.
3. If you have an open side, Engineering's crew numbers tick down. Get out, or SEAL and VENT.
4. With a damaged Garuda, lure it through the belt before boarding.

- **Rules it uses.** The gas cloud's box. The false-dot rule (built in Steps 148 and 151, removed in 157). The
  radiation condition (spec §13.3). Boarding odds.
- **Fixes.** FINDERS KEEPERS and boarding are one coin flip today (58%, P1). Radiation gives a "soften it
  first" step. Dead time: something to read on the way.
- **Size.** Medium.
- **Sums.** False contacts at 2 a sweep, each lasting 8 s: about 4 on screen at gain 3 (sweep 3.9 s), 5 to 6
  at gain 5 (sweep 2.9 s). Boarding: defenders are a quarter of the enemy's crew (`HangarSystem.cs`). 52 crew
  gives 13 defenders, 18 marines win 58%. Two leaking rooms for 3 minutes kill 18: 34 crew, 9 defenders, 67%.
  At 26 crew: 7 defenders, 72%.
- **Toy test.** See the flicker, wait one sweep, pick the real one. The commitment is going in with an open
  side. The tension is reading a screen you can't fully trust, which is Red October's sonar in a nutshell.

#### C3 · Hot wrecks (small)

Wrecks of a dead fleet (GDD p.7) whose reactors still burn. They give off THERM only. Working ships give off
THERM and RAD.

1. Two red bars on THERM.
2. Flip to RAD. One bar goes. That one is a wreck.
3. Fire at the other, or scan it.

- **Rules it uses.** DRADIS bands. Each contact already has its list of bands (`TacticalWorld.AddContact`).
  Wrecks already exist (Step 131).
- **Fixes.** S2 in NO PEEKING, where no scan is allowed: put one hot wreck 4,000 away near the Garuda. Firing
  straight then hits the right one about half the time (guess), unless you flip the band or watch which bar
  moves. The extra step is rewarded, not the shortcut. In WOLF HUNT, a false first contact: is it the Garuda,
  or a wreck you'd waste a loud scan on?
- **Size.** Small.
- **Toy test.** One flip of a switch, one bar gone. Small and satisfying.

#### C4 · Clouds both ways (medium, after QA finding 2)

The Garuda can wait in a gas cloud, by our rule: we hear it only at 30% there, and see it only inside 400.
Today clouds hide only us ("Enemies don't hide in clouds yet", `TacticalWorld.cs` line 158), and the enemy has
no signal at all (QA, finding 2).

- **Fixes.** The run to the jump point (P1: 0–25% of good players die after the kill) gets a real question:
  is that cloud empty? It keeps the owner's rule: one rule, both ways.
- **Sums.** If its signal is 100, we hear it in a cloud only inside 6,250 × 0.3 = 1,875. That is inside its
  railgun reach.
- **Size.** Medium, because the enemy needs a signal first.

#### C5 · Rocks stop slugs (small)

A slug that meets a rock stops there, for every ship.

- **Fixes.** S4. It makes "behind a rock" true cover both ways, and turns rock fields into lanes and corners.
  For the nose gun, it means long shots need a clear lane, and the enemy knows where the lanes are.
- **Rules it uses.** Ships are solid boxes and nothing passes through anything (Step 123). Shots already test
  boxes; rocks already have boxes for crashes. The GDD's penetration rule (GDD p.49) can come later.
- **Size.** Small.

## 3. The nose gun ("Nuclear Lance"), under discussion

Not agreed and not built. "No spinal railgun" is on the Ruled out list; the owner reopened it on 2026-11-08
(`SNIPER_PLAN.md`). Numbers only.

**Does it make a winning strategy?** The plan's rule: a hit from outside the 15,000 flare tells the Garuda only
the direction, and it searches down that line. So after the first hit it comes at you bow on.

- Bow hits strike PROW: 150 armour, the magazine behind it. They never reach the reactor (behind PORT). Six
  PORT hits kill it (P1). So the nose gun can't kill a Garuda from range.
- Five bow hits stop its guns: three open the 150 armour at 50 each, two take the magazine from 5 to 1, and
  below 3 nothing loads (spec §13.3).
- A bow is narrow. Its half width is 62.5. Our bow turns in whole degrees, so the aim is off by up to half a
  degree: ±87 at 10,000, ±131 at 15,000 (P1's ±87 checks). Bow-on hit chance (guess, the aim error spread
  evenly): 72% at 10,000, 48% at 15,000, 36% at 20,000.
- My run (20,000 tries each): the Garuda comes from 15,000 at 28 (SEARCHING) and needs 464 s to reach 2,000.
  With a shot every 20, 30 or 45 s (guess), five bow hits land first every time, in a median 160, 240 or
  315 s. From 20,000: 220, 300 or 405 s, against 643 s.

So by the sums: no kill from range, but the Garuda arrives with its guns down. That could make the close fight
one-sided. The Garuda's damage party repairs the magazine (spec §13.6), and how fast it does decides how strong this is.
Player 1 case 6.

**Two risks.**

1. **Hit, hide, repeat.** If a long hit ever stops starting the reinforcements' countdown, or the Garuda
   gives up after 90 s and goes back to its route side-on, the player gets one free PORT shot per cycle.
   Armour never comes back. Today any hit makes it ALERT, which starts the countdown (Rule 5.4.a). Keep that.
2. **Fires leak our position.** QA finding 3: a fire on the Garuda counts as a new hit every 30 s and tells it
   where we are within 15%, from anywhere. That would undo the flare rule. Fix it first.

**What would make the player think twice.** Frigates: bow-on at 5,000 a frigate (half width 15.6) is hit
about 36% of the time, 18% at 10,000 (guess, same sum). Enemy listening buoys (C1) inside the flare circle:
they hear the flare and call the Garuda to your spot. Rocks that stop slugs (C5): long shots need a lane.
