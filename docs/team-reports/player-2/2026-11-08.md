**What drives it.**

1. **Knowing its course doesn't help the railgun.** A lock with no course aims at where the Garuda is now.
   That slug misses only if the Garuda moves more than 219 units across our line while it flies. 219 is
   half its hit box length: 350 × 1.25 ÷ 2. At any heading, a miss needs speed × flight time > 219. The
   slug flies at 300, so it can't miss inside 219 × 300 ÷ speed:

   | Garuda speed | No-course shot can't miss inside |
   |---|---|
   | 35 (top) | 1,875 |
   | 22 (cruise) | 2,983 |
   | 14 (side-on in the fight) | 4,688 |

   even hurts (Player 1, finding 4). Fire, close and quiet differ only in how loud the approach is. That
   is why firing straight ties for best with experts. A scan still names the rooms you hit, and it helps
   torpedoes.
2. **In the chases a scan costs nothing.** The Garuda hears us out to (4,375 + 625 × its gain) × our signal

   power, so it hears us at 5,312. At signal 60 + 35 it hears us at 5,938. Both are inside our contact range of

3. **FINDERS KEEPERS** comes down to the boarding roll: 18 marines against 13 defenders, 18 ÷ 31 = 58%.

**Smallest change.**

**What drives it: the face.** Let p be the share of fights where the Garuda turns PORT to us first. Then

good player gets about 10%. This assumes good and expert players do equally well once the face is set.
The expert at x5 doesn't fit the formula: PORT first 62–83%, but only 40% wins. At x5 slow hands lose
even a PORT fight.

## 3. Doubts about Player 1's numbers

1. **The numbers file and the report disagree in 11 cases.** These are the cases Player 1 played 48 more
   times. The report has the 72-fight results. The file still has the 24-fight ones, and the producer
   will copy the file into the Sheet. The biggest gaps:

   | INTERCEPT good x1 close | 29 | 21 |
   | COLD RUN expert x1 quiet | 50 | 42 |
   | WOLF HUNT expert x5 quiet | 46 | 42 |

   The rest differ by 2 points.
2. **Radio timing.** The calculator sends a message every 120 s (60 on air + 60). The game starts its 60 s
   wait when the last message is read or lost (`EnemyBrain.cs`, `Send`, lines 156–160). A good player who
   decodes in 15 s gets one about every 75 s, and an expert about every 68 s. So dead time in WOLF HUNT and
   INTERCEPT should be roughly 70–90 s, not 120, and the radio gives fresher courses than the maths
   allows. The report also says "every 60 s" in one place and "every 2 minutes" in another.
3. **Experts aim worse than good players.** At x1, hits ÷ slugs from the file: good 97–100%, expert 61–85%.
   At x5: expert 81–88%. Something in the expert's script spoils its own shots: weaving, or backing in
   reverse. So expert win rates may be too low.
4. **Expert x5 gets PORT first 62–83%, against 17–29% at x1.** At x5 the board's step is 2.1 s, so the
   Garuda turns up to 29° in one step. The side it ends on probably comes from the step size, not skill.
   The game has the same step (QA finding 4), so this is real in the game too. But it means the expert
   x5 wins measure the step, not the player.
5. **"The Garuda's 45–50 shots" at x5.** It carries 2 × 22 = 44 slugs. The count must include torpedoes (4)
   or frigates. Please say which.
6. **Our hit box.** The report says ±156 along our length. The file gives 200 × 1.25 ÷ 2 = ±125, and the
   calculator uses ±125. This is only a slip in the text. The dodge result stands: the Garuda's spread is
   ±79 at 1,500.
7. **FINDERS KEEPERS: "62% in all 36 cases" is one result, not 36.** Every case uses the same seeds, so the
   boarding roll comes out the same each time. The true chance is 18 ÷ 31 = 58%. 24 fights are good to
   about ±20.
8. **Seed sets that disagree.** 11 of 216 cases differed by more than 25 points. That is what chance gives:
   two sets of 12 at 50% differ by more than 25 points 22% of the time. So it isn't a bug. But it means 24
   fights can't settle the choices or the win rate to better than about 20 points. The cases that matter
   need about 100 fights (±10).
9. **Two columns are missing from the numbers file.** The calculator works out the time of the first shot
   and the dead time after contact, but neither is in the file. Without them I can't check setting up for
   each first move.
10. **Decoding at x5.** A good player's 15 real seconds is 75 game seconds, and a message is on air for 60. If
    the player has not locked the signal by then, the game loses the message. The calculator doesn't check.
11. **The new player's search power decides their 0%.** ENG 5, SEN 3, WEP 2 is a guess, and it gives signal
    100. Unalerted, the Garuda then hears out to 6,250, the same as our DRADIS reach. So it hears a new
    player the moment the player sees it. A new player who follows the help text ("keep your power low")
